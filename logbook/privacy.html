Logbook Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-22

Your data belongs to you. In this policy, we lay out what data is processed and why, how it is handled, and your rights. This policy applies to Logbook, its support pages and correspondence with Creatrix. Contact: creatrix.studio26@gmail.com.

Workout logs, routines, exercise settings and app settings are stored only on your device and are never sent to Creatrix or any third party. This does not mean there is no off-device collection: if you allow it, app-improvement statistics and crash reports process the service data described below.

What we collect and why

Identity and access

Logbook does not require an app account, sign-in or account sync. SDKs can generate their own identifiers, so the absence of an account does not make all service data anonymous.

Billing information

Logbook offers one optional lifetime purchase: a one-time payment through the Apple App Store or Google Play. Apple and Google handle payments through StoreKit and Play Billing. The app receives only whether the purchase is owned, to unlock the features it covers. It does not collect card numbers, bank details or billing addresses. The stores retain their own transaction records under their policies.

Product interactions and local content

The app stores your workout logs (the sets you log), routines, workout and exercise history, exercise settings and app settings in app-private storage. The app has no Creatrix-operated upload, account-sync or cloud-backup service for this content. Android system cloud backup and device-to-device transfer of app data are turned off.

The app’s custom analytics code does not send workout numbers or anything you type to providers. It does not automatically collect your contacts, photos, messages, browsing history or precise GPS location.

CSV import: when you pick a file exported from another app (for example Strong, Hevy or JEFIT), the app reads and converts it on your device only. The file is not sent to Creatrix or anyone else.

Backup, restore and CSV export: for a backup or CSV export, the app writes a file on your device and hands it to the system share sheet or save picker. Where the file goes is your choice; the app does not receive a copy. Restore reads a backup file that you pick. The app makes no automatic or cloud backup. Recipients’ handling of the copies you share is outside the app’s local storage controls.

Your choice comes first: on first launch the app asks before sending anything. Nothing described below is sent until you tap Allow, in every region; in the EU/EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the form shows the switch off by default. One choice covers both app-improvement statistics and crash reports. You can turn it off at any time in Settings → Privacy; turning it off stops sending and deletes statistics waiting on the device.

Usage analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase, GA4): off until you allow it. We use it to see which steps of the app work and where people stop. When it is on, Google receives which screens are opened and which buttons are tapped, and whether imports and backups succeeded, as coarse counts or ranges, with no workout numbers and no free text, together with app and device information. Google’s SDK can process app-instance identifiers, IP-derived region and diagnostics. The data is not used for advertising, and the app collects no advertising ID (iOS IDFA or Android advertising ID). These settings do not eliminate all SDK identifiers or diagnostics.

Crash reports (Sentry): follow the same choice as usage analytics: off until you allow it. Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., United States) receives crash and app-hang reports with the device model, OS version, app version, the screen open at the time and the error, used only to fix problems. They do not include your IP address or personal identifiers, screenshots or screen recordings, workout numbers or anything you type, and are not linked to your identity or used for tracking.

Key app events (Creatrix server): so that no important step is lost, key events (such as a finished workout or a finished import) are also sent to Creatrix’s own server, under the same choice. Each event carries its name, coarse counts, the time, a random event ID used only to remove duplicates, the app version, platform and system version. It carries no account or device identifier, workout numbers or anything you type. Like any server, it receives your IP address when the app connects, but the IP address is not stored with the event or in server logs; it is held in memory for up to 24 hours only to limit request rates, and is never written to disk (see “General geolocation data”). Events waiting to be sent are kept on the device for up to 30 days, and are deleted when you turn the choice off.

General geolocation data

Network providers receive IP addresses when their services are contacted. Google Analytics can process approximate region. The app reads the region setting of your device, without location permission, to decide which consent form to show. The app does not request location permission or supply GPS coordinates to these SDKs.

Website interactions

These support pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. They contain no analytics, advertising or replay scripts, tracking pixels or embedded forms added by Creatrix. GitHub processes visitors’ connection information, including IP addresses, under the GitHub Privacy Statement. Following external links exposes connection information to those destinations under their policies.

Free features, lifetime purchase and no ads

The free features stay free. The optional lifetime purchase unlocks the features described in the app, such as trend charts and more than two routines. The app shows no ads: it contains no third-party advertising SDK, uses no advertising identifier and makes no ad-network requests. It does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites for advertising.

When usage analytics is on, screen and button use and import or backup outcomes can be measured without your workout content. This does not enable advertising storage or ad personalization. Google/Firebase SDK data is described above. See Firebase privacy information.

These pages do not set Creatrix cookies. In the native app, preferences, consent and purchase state are stored locally; third-party SDK storage and identifiers are governed by the relevant service settings and policies.

Voluntary correspondence

When you email Creatrix with a question or ask for help, we receive your email address and whatever message, attachments or diagnostic details you choose to send. We use that correspondence to respond and investigate the issue, and may keep it as a history of the request. The app does not automatically attach your workout logs or backups. Please send only information needed for your request.

When we access or disclose your information

To provide services you request: Google/Firebase handles the usage analytics described above and Sentry the crash reports. Key app events are stored for Creatrix by Supabase (database), Google Cloud (server) and Cloudflare (network, which passes the connection through), which act as processors and do not use them for their own purposes. Apple and Google handle store transactions, and GitHub hosts these pages. They receive the service data described above and may process it under their own terms. We do not have remote access to your workout logs.

At your direction: backups, CSV exports, system sharing and email send content only when you initiate the action. For troubleshooting, we work with information you voluntarily provide; contact us before sending sensitive files. We do not sell your workout logs or provide them for ad targeting.

To protect rights or comply with law: we may preserve or disclose information actually in our possession when necessary to comply with applicable legal process or to address unlawful use, fraud or threats to safety. We cannot disclose local content we have never received. A business transfer involving information we hold would require applicable legal safeguards and notice. There is no blanket permission to transfer your workout logs.

Your rights with respect to your information

Depending on applicable law, you may have rights to know about processing, access, correct, delete or receive a copy of your information, restrict or object to processing, withdraw consent, and complain to a privacy authority. Applicable rights relating to sale/sharing, automated decisions, non-discrimination or an appeal also remain available. We do not use your workout logs to make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects about you. Some information may be exempt from a request under applicable law.

View, edit, back up, export or delete your workout logs in the app. In every region, Settings → Privacy lets you turn off app-improvement statistics and crash reports at any time. Turning it off stops future sending and deletes statistics waiting on the device; it does not delete information already received by providers or stop separate store processing. Device and provider settings offer additional controls.

For information held by us, deletion of statistics or crash reports already sent, or help with a provider request, email creatrix.studio26@gmail.com. We may need reasonable proof of identity or an agent’s authority and enough information to locate data. No app account links every provider identifier to your email, so we may not be able to identify all SDK records. We will explain available steps and applicable limits rather than promise erasure we cannot verify. You may also contact Apple, Google, Sentry or GitHub under their privacy policies.

How we secure your data

Workout logs and related local data use the operating system’s app sandbox and the backup settings described above. The app does not add its own encryption layer to its local data. Keep your device secured and updated; anyone with access to an unlocked device or an exported or backup file may be able to read its contents. Providers document security measures such as encrypted transport for their services. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, and this policy does not promise universal end-to-end encryption or protection from every loss.

What happens when you delete content

Delete all data in Settings removes your workout logs, routines, exercise settings and app settings from the device. Uninstalling the app also removes everything on the device (on iOS, delete the app rather than offloading it). Save a backup first if you need one. Removed data cannot be restored by us. There is no cloud account to delete or recover from.

Local deletion does not erase backup or export files you saved elsewhere, store transactions or data already sent to providers. To ask for deletion of statistics or crash reports already sent, email creatrix.studio26@gmail.com.

Data retention

Local data remains until you delete it or remove the app. Key app events are kept for 1 year from receipt and then deleted by a daily cleanup. Google Analytics keeps usage analytics for 14 months. Sentry keeps crash and app-hang reports for up to 90 days. To ask us to delete data sooner, email us the approximate time you used the app. We keep email correspondence for the time necessary to handle the request and meet legal obligations, resolve disputes or enforce agreements.

Google, Sentry, Apple and GitHub retain service records under their own policies and configured service settings. Apart from the periods above, we do not promise a particular SDK retention period or automatic provider deletion after uninstalling. Contact us for assistance with a request; provider retention and deletion scope may differ from local deletion.

Location of site and data

Workout logs are processed on your device. Key app events are stored in the United States: Supabase region us-west-2 (Oregon) and Google Cloud region us-west1 (Oregon), reached through Cloudflare’s network. Crash reports sent to Sentry are stored in the United States. Data sent to Google, Apple, GitHub or an email provider may be processed outside your country, including in the United States and other countries where those providers operate. We do not claim that all data stays in Korea or in a particular provider region.

International transfers

Usage analytics (overseas transfer under the Korean Personal Information Protection Act):

  • Items: screens opened, buttons tapped, import and backup outcomes as coarse counts or ranges, and the app and device information and SDK identifiers described above.
  • Country, time and method: the United States and other countries where Google operates; sent over an encrypted HTTPS connection while you use the app, only after you tap Allow.
  • Recipient: Google LLC (Google Analytics for Firebase), see Firebase privacy information.
  • Purpose and retention: measuring which steps of the app work, for 14 months.
  • How to refuse: do not tap Allow, or turn it off in Settings → Privacy; every app feature still works.

Key app events (overseas transfer under the Korean Personal Information Protection Act):

  • Items: the event name and coarse counts, time, random event ID, app version, platform and system version described above.
  • Country, time and method: the United States; sent over an encrypted HTTPS connection after a key event, when the app opens, or when it retries saved events.
  • Recipients: Supabase, Inc. (database, us-west-2), see the Supabase Privacy Policy; Google LLC (Google Cloud server, us-west1), see the Google Privacy Policy; Cloudflare, Inc. (network), see the Cloudflare Privacy Policy.
  • Purpose and retention: measuring which steps of the app work, for 1 year from receipt.
  • How to refuse: do not tap Allow, or turn it off in Settings → Privacy; every app feature still works.

Crash and app-hang reports (overseas transfer under the Korean Personal Information Protection Act):

  • Items: the error, app version, device model, OS version and the name of the screen open at the time, described above.
  • Country, time and method: the United States; sent over an encrypted HTTPS connection after a crash or hang, usually the next time the app opens.
  • Recipient: Functional Software, Inc. (Sentry), see the Sentry Privacy Policy.
  • Purpose and retention: finding and fixing crashes and freezes, for up to 90 days from receipt.
  • How to refuse: do not tap Allow, or turn it off in Settings → Privacy; crash reports stop with app-improvement statistics, and every app feature still works.

Applicable privacy and international-transfer requirements remain in force, including any required notices, legal grounds, consent or safeguards for information sent abroad. Provider policies describe their transfer practices. We do not claim that Creatrix has adopted Basecamp’s data processing agreement or its contractual transfer arrangements, and use of the app is not a substitute for any consent separately required by law. Contact us for information relevant to your request.

Children

Logbook is not intended for children under 13, or under the minimum age required in their country if higher (14 in Korea, up to 16 in some EU countries). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has used the app and information was sent to a provider, contact creatrix.studio26@gmail.com and we will help remove it where we can.

Changes and questions

We may update this policy as needed to comply with relevant regulations and reflect new practices. Whenever we make a significant change, we will refresh the date at the top of this page and take appropriate steps to notify users, including obtaining consent where required.

Have questions, comments or concerns about this policy, your data or your rights? Email creatrix.studio26@gmail.com. The English version prevails if translations differ, except where Korean law requires otherwise.
